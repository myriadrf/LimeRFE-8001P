Overview
########

LimeRFE 8001P is a two channel RF front-end module. Its block diagram is shown in Figure 3. Please note that the diagram depicts the RF section of the board in detail and all other peripheral circuits are only referenced.

.. figure:: /images/LimeRFE-8001P_v1.1_BD.png
  :width: 600

  Figure 3: LimeRFE 8001P v1.1 board block diagram

LimeRFE 8001P channels are designated as CH#1 and CH#2 and will be referenced as such throughout the rest of the document. Each channel is independent and perform the same function with exception of some minor differences in frequency conversion sub-blocks, described further in this document. All solid lines represent default electrical connections between sub-blocks and dashed lines denote optional connections.

The board consists of two channels where each channel consists of the following key sub-blocks:

* Amplification blocks (various amplifiers in transmitter and receiver chains:, PDA, DA and LNA sub-blocks)
* Linearization block (SC1905)
* Frequency conversion block (LMS8001B)

The overall functionality could be divided as follows:

Transmitter

* amplification blocks (some of the amplification blocks could be bypassed)
* linearization block (by default this is part of the transmitter function but could be bypassed by 0 Ohm resistors)
* frequency conversion block

Receiver

* amplification block
* frequency conversion block

Observation

* frequency conversion block
* linearization block (by default this is a part of the observation path but could be bypassed by 0 Ohm resistors)

The transmitter, receiver and observation blocks are described in :doc:`txblock`, :doc:`rxblock` and :doc:`orxblock`.

.. _frequency-conversion:

Frequency Conversion
====================

The frequency conversion block of each channel is built around the `LMS8001B`_ chip (U1 for CH#1, U2 for CH#2), a highly linear 4-channel frequency shifter.
Input signals of the transmitter are in the IF range (1.8 – 2.0 GHz), and output signals are in the RF range (3.3 – 5.0 GHz).

.. note::
   Strictly speaking, both the IF and RF signals of the frequency conversion block are in the RF range. In this documentation, IF refers to the lower frequency band and RF to the higher frequency band. The two bands are separated by the frequency conversion block.

Three of the four LMS8001B channels are used:

* channel B (*Ch.B*) – receiver signals,
* channel C (*Ch.C*) – transmitter signals,
* channel D (*Ch.D*) – observation signals, i.e. signals coupled from the output of the amplifier to be linearized.

Channel A (*Ch.A*) is reserved for debugging and is not used in normal operation. On CH#1, *Ch.A* is designed for up-conversion (J33 input, J34 output). On CH#2, *Ch.A* is designed for down-conversion (J35 input, J57 output).

The LO signal for the LMS8001B is generated by its internal PLL. An external LO signal can be applied to J3 (CH#1) or J26 (CH#2).
